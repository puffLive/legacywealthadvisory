Your ability to earn an income is often your largest financial asset. If an illness or injury kept you from working, how would your household cover its bills? Two kinds of insurance are built for this risk, and they work in very different ways.

## Disability insurance

Disability insurance pays a **monthly benefit** if you cannot work because of illness or injury, as defined in your policy. Key features to understand include:

- **Waiting period:** how long you must be disabled before benefits begin, often 90 days or more.
- **Benefit period:** how long benefits can continue, such as two years, five years or to age 65.
- **Definition of disability:** whether you must be unable to do *your own* occupation or *any* occupation you are reasonably suited for.

### Group versus individual coverage

Many employees have **group long-term disability** through work. If the employer pays the premiums, benefits are generally **taxable**. Individual coverage that you pay for personally generally provides **tax-free** benefits. Group coverage also typically ends when you leave the employer, while individual coverage stays with you.

Government programs such as **CPP disability benefits** have strict eligibility criteria and modest benefit amounts, so they are rarely enough on their own.

## Critical illness insurance

Critical illness insurance pays a **one-time, tax-free lump sum** if you are diagnosed with a covered condition, such as certain cancers, heart attack or stroke, and you survive a waiting period set out in the policy, often 30 days.

You can use the money however you choose, for example to:

- replace a spouse's income while they care for you,
- pay for treatments or care not covered by provincial health plans,
- pay down debt, or
- keep a business running while you recover.

Each policy defines which conditions are covered and what must happen for a claim to be paid, so the details matter.

## How they work together

| | Disability insurance | Critical illness insurance |
|---|---|---|
| How it pays | Monthly income | One lump sum |
| What triggers it | Inability to work | Diagnosis of a covered condition |
| Main purpose | Replace lost income | Cover costs and give flexibility |

Some people need one, some need both, and the right amounts depend on your income, savings, debts, family and any coverage you already have.

## Questions to ask about your coverage

- How much of my income would I receive, and would it be taxable?
- How long would I wait before benefits start, and how long would they last?
- Does my coverage end if I change jobs?
- Which conditions are covered, and which are excluded?

Insurance is subject to underwriting and the terms of each contract. Reviewing your coverage with a licensed advisor can show where the gaps are before you need it.
