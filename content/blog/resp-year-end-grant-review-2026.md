Saving for a child's education is about more than tuition. It is about giving the next generation options, whether they pursue university, college or eligible trades training.

As December 31 approaches, families may find it useful to review their Registered Education Savings Plans (RESPs).

## Understand the government grant

The basic Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG) matches 20% of eligible contributions, normally up to $500 per year per beneficiary. For example, $2,500 in eligible contributions could attract $500 in basic grants. Depending on family income, additional grants may be available. The combined lifetime CESG ceiling is $7,200 per beneficiary.

## Review missed grant opportunities

Unused basic grant room can carry forward. With sufficient unused room, eligible contributions of $5,000 in one calendar year could attract $1,000 in basic CESG. Additional CESG does not carry forward. These examples assume eligibility and sufficient remaining lifetime grant room.

## Pay attention to age-related deadlines

Grant eligibility generally ends in the calendar year a child turns 17. For grants at ages 16 and 17, specific earlier savings conditions apply: by the end of the year the child turns 15, either at least $2,000 must have been contributed and not withdrawn, or at least $100 must have been contributed and not withdrawn in each of four earlier qualifying years.

Families approaching these milestones may wish to confirm their records and their provider's year-end processing deadlines.

## Coordinate contributions across the family

There is no annual statutory RESP contribution ceiling, but the lifetime contribution limit is $50,000 per beneficiary across all plans. Parents and grandparents can coordinate their records to avoid excess contributions. RESP contributions are not tax-deductible.

## Think beyond the grant

Investment growth is generally tax-deferred inside the plan. Educational assistance payments containing grants and earnings are generally taxable to the student; original contributions can generally be returned tax-free, subject to plan and grant rules.

The investment approach, time until enrolment, household cash flow and other financial priorities all deserve consideration. Education savings are one part of a broader family financial plan.
