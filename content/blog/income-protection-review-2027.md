A financial plan can look strong when income arrives regularly. But what happens to mortgage payments, education savings and retirement contributions if illness or injury interrupts that income?

Before entering 2027, consider five questions.

## 1. How much does your household need each month?

Separate essential expenses from discretionary spending. Include housing, groceries, utilities, debt payments and insurance. This creates a starting point for assessing how much income your household depends on.

## 2. How long could accessible savings support you?

Imagine essential expenses of $4,000 per month and accessible savings of $12,000. Without other income, those savings would cover approximately three months, assuming expenses remain unchanged. This is an illustration, not a recommended emergency fund amount.

## 3. What does your workplace coverage actually provide?

Disability insurance can replace part of your earnings if you meet the policy's disability definition. Review the monthly maximum, waiting period, benefit duration, exclusions and possible reductions for other benefits. Employer-funded disability coverage generally produces taxable benefits; benefits are generally tax-free when the employee pays the entire premium.

## 4. What happens if recovery creates additional costs?

Critical illness insurance typically provides a lump sum when a covered diagnosis meets the contract requirements. It serves a different purpose from recurring disability income. Coverage definitions and claim conditions vary. Neither type of insurance guarantees payment for every illness.

## 5. What if you own a business?

Personal living costs and business overhead are separate obligations. Consider who would handle client relationships, payroll and ongoing commitments during an absence. Review available cash reserves, existing insurance and continuity arrangements with qualified professionals.

## Build and protect together

Growing wealth matters, but so does protecting the income that makes growth possible. Reviewing existing coverage and cash flow can help identify gaps before an unexpected event.
