# RRSP Planning Before 2027: Five Questions Worth Asking

As the year draws to a close, Canadians often focus on one question: *How much should I contribute to my RRSP?* A more useful starting point may be whether the contribution fits your overall financial plan.

A registered retirement savings plan (RRSP) can be an important tool for retirement savings. Eligible contributions may reduce taxable income, while investment income generally grows tax-deferred until withdrawn. But contribution room, tax timing, future withdrawals and other priorities all matter.

Here are five questions worth reviewing.

## 1. Do you know your actual RRSP contribution room?

Your personal RRSP deduction limit is not automatically the annual maximum published by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). For 2026, the annual RRSP dollar limit is $33,810, but an individual's limit depends on factors including previous earned income, unused room and pension adjustments.

Check your latest CRA notice of assessment or reassessment and your CRA account. Also account for contributions already made that may not yet appear in CRA records. Your deduction limit and your available contribution room are not necessarily identical.

## 2. Are you confusing the calendar year with the RRSP deadline?

For the 2026 tax year, eligible RRSP contributions can generally be made through **March 1, 2027**, subject to applicable contribution and deduction rules. That means December 31 is not the usual final deadline for an RRSP contribution intended for a 2026 deduction.

However, waiting until the deadline can leave less time to verify room, review investments and resolve administrative issues. The date that matters for a particular decision may also differ for people approaching the age limit for RRSP contributions.

## 3. Have you considered your workplace pension?

Participation in an employer pension plan may reduce the RRSP room you build for future years through a pension adjustment. Reviewing your pension statement alongside your CRA information can help prevent an unrealistic assumption about how much additional RRSP room you have.

The objective is to understand how all retirement income sources work together, not to assess your RRSP in isolation.

## 4. Would an RRSP contribution fit your cash-flow priorities?

A potential tax deduction is valuable, but it does not automatically make an RRSP contribution the best use of every available dollar. Emergency savings, expensive debt, a shorter-term purchase, a TFSA or an FHSA may also deserve attention depending on the circumstances and eligibility rules.

Money withdrawn from an RRSP is generally taxable, except where specific programs and conditions apply. Considering the likely timing and taxation of future withdrawals is part of sound retirement planning.

## 5. Have you reviewed your investments, not just your contribution amount?

An RRSP is an account structure, not an investment strategy. The investments held inside it should reflect the intended retirement horizon, capacity to accept losses, income needs and overall asset allocation.

Before year-end, it may be useful to review whether your investment mix remains appropriate and whether your RRSP complements other savings, insurance and estate-planning arrangements.

## The bigger picture

Retirement planning is about more than maximizing a single tax deduction. It is about coordinating savings, taxes, investments, retirement income and protection so that decisions made today support the future you want to build.
