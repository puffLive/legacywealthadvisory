If you are leaving an employer, or retiring from one, that offers a **defined benefit (DB) pension**, you may be asked to choose between keeping a monthly pension and taking the **commuted value** as a lump sum. It is one of the largest financial decisions many people make, and it usually cannot be reversed.

## What is a commuted value?

The commuted value is the amount the pension plan calculates today to represent the future pension you have earned. It is based on your pension formula, your age, and current interest rates and mortality assumptions. When interest rates are lower, commuted values tend to be higher, and the reverse is also true.

## Where the money goes

If you take the commuted value, it is not normally paid to you as cash. Typically:

- a portion is transferred to a **locked-in retirement account** (such as a LIRA), which has limits on how and when you can withdraw, and
- any amount above the maximum transfer allowed under the *Income Tax Act* is paid to you as **taxable cash**, which may be sheltered if you have unused RRSP room.

That taxable portion can be significant, so it is worth estimating the tax before you decide.

## Reasons people keep the monthly pension

- **Predictable income for life**, no matter how long you live.
- **No investment risk** for you. The plan carries it.
- **Survivor benefits** for a spouse or partner, if elected.
- **Indexing**, if your plan increases payments with inflation.

## Reasons people choose the commuted value

- **Control and flexibility** over how the money is invested and withdrawn.
- **Estate value.** Money left in a LIRA or the resulting income fund can pass to beneficiaries, while many monthly pensions stop on death or continue only to a spouse.
- **Health or family circumstances** that make a long retirement less likely.
- Concerns about the **financial health** of the pension plan or the employer.

## Questions to work through

1. How long might you, and your spouse, live?
2. Is the pension indexed to inflation, and by how much?
3. How comfortable are you managing investments through market ups and downs?
4. How much of the commuted value would be paid as taxable cash?
5. What other income will you have, such as CPP, OAS and personal savings?
6. What do you want to leave to your family?

## A decision worth modelling

There is no answer that suits everyone. The best way to compare the options is to model both paths side by side, using your actual pension statement, before the election deadline your plan gives you.
