NVIDIA (NASDAQ: NVDA) remains a central company in the global artificial intelligence investment story. Its graphics processors, networking technology and software ecosystem help power AI training and inference in data centres. But an impressive business and an attractive investment are not always the same thing: the price an investor pays and the risks they accept matter.

## NVIDIA's latest quarterly results

On **November 17, 2026**, NVIDIA announced results for its **third quarter of fiscal 2027**, which ended October 25, 2026. The company reported:

- **Revenue of US$115.8 billion**, up **103%** from the same quarter a year earlier and **20%** from the preceding quarter.
- **Data Center revenue of US$107.6 billion**, up **110%** year over year.
- **GAAP gross margin of 74.8%**.
- **GAAP diluted earnings per share of US$2.70**.

For the following quarter, management forecast revenue of approximately **US$130 billion, plus or minus 3%**. The company noted that its fourth fiscal quarter includes an extra week. These are company-reported figures, not guarantees of future results.

Source: [NVIDIA Q3 fiscal 2027 earnings release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx).

## Why investors are watching the AI buildout

Large cloud operators and other organizations continue to invest in computing capacity to train and run AI models. NVIDIA benefits from demand for accelerators and the surrounding systems, networking and software needed to operate them at scale.

The company reported that its **Vera Rubin NVL72** platform had begun shipping. New products can support growth, but they also create execution demands involving manufacturing, customer adoption, power availability and the pace of data-centre construction.

## Four risks that deserve attention

### 1. Expectations and valuation

Rapid revenue growth does not automatically make a stock inexpensive. If investors have already priced in years of exceptional growth, even strong earnings can disappoint the market. An assessment of NVDA should consider valuation at the time of purchase, rather than relying on historical returns.

### 2. Dependence on AI capital spending

Data-centre customers may adjust their budgets if AI projects deliver slower-than-expected returns, financing costs rise or infrastructure constraints delay construction. A slowdown in spending could affect suppliers across the AI ecosystem.

### 3. Competition and technology change

NVIDIA faces competition from other semiconductor companies and custom chips designed by major customers. Its software ecosystem is an important competitive advantage, but no advantage should be assumed permanent.

### 4. Policy, supply-chain and geopolitical exposure

Export restrictions, access to advanced manufacturing, trade policy and geopolitical tensions can influence where NVIDIA sells products and how quickly it can meet demand. NVIDIA's latest outlook did not assume Data Center compute revenue from China.

## What this means for Canadian investors

Canadian investors should consider **portfolio concentration** before adding NVDA. Exposure may already exist through U.S. equity funds, global technology funds or broad-market index funds. Holding the stock directly on top of those investments can increase dependence on one company and one investment theme.

**Currency risk** matters too. NVIDIA shares trade in U.S. dollars, so changes in the Canadian dollar can affect returns measured in Canadian dollars. Account selection, time horizon, liquidity needs and overall risk tolerance should also inform any investment decision.

A practical approach is to review the total exposure to NVIDIA and the broader AI theme across all accounts, establish an appropriate diversification policy and rebalance when allocations drift away from the plan. None of these steps requires predicting next week's stock price.

## The bottom line

NVIDIA's November 2026 earnings demonstrate remarkable current demand for AI infrastructure. The longer-term investment question is whether that demand, profitability and competitive position can justify the stock's valuation over time. Investors should distinguish **business momentum** from **future shareholder returns**.
