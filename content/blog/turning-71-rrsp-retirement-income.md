Building retirement savings is only half the journey. Turning those savings into sustainable income requires a different set of decisions.

## 1. Know your year-end deadline

If you turn 71 in 2026, your RRSP must mature by December 31, 2026. The principal options are withdrawing the savings, transferring them directly to a Registered Retirement Income Fund (RRIF), or purchasing an eligible annuity. A qualifying direct transfer or annuity purchase generally avoids immediate taxation, whereas a cash withdrawal is generally taxable.

## 2. Understand how a RRIF works

A RRIF allows investments to remain tax-deferred until money is paid out. Mandatory minimum payments begin in the calendar year following establishment. Withdrawals are generally taxable income, and amounts above the minimum can also be withdrawn.

When opening a RRIF, you may elect to calculate the minimum using your spouse's or common-law partner's age. This election cannot subsequently be changed for that RRIF.

## 3. Consider the role of an annuity

An eligible annuity can convert savings into contractual income payments. Compare payment guarantees, survivor provisions, liquidity restrictions and inflation exposure before making a decision. Contract terms vary.

## 4. Coordinate all your retirement income

Consider workplace pensions, CPP, OAS, registered savings and other assets together. A withdrawal strategy should account for household expenses, income taxes, unexpected costs and the possibility of a long retirement.

## 5. Review your estate arrangements

Review beneficiary or successor annuitant designations where available, and coordinate them with your will and estate plan. Provincial law, contract terms and tax rules can affect the outcome.

## Build a retirement income plan with purpose

The goal is not simply to meet a withdrawal deadline. It is to understand how your savings can support your lifestyle over time. Financial institutions may have processing deadlines before December 31, so allow time to review your options.
