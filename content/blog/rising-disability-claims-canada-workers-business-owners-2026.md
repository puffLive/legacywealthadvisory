# Are Rising Disability Claims a Warning Sign for Canadian Workers and Business Owners?

**November 22, 2026 | Legacy Wealth Advisory**

What would happen to your household finances—or your business—if an illness or injury prevented you from working for several months?

That question deserves renewed attention following third-quarter 2026 results from some of Canada's largest insurers. Recent financial reports point to pressure in parts of the group disability insurance market. The lesson is not that every Canadian worker faces a greater personal risk today, or that every employer's premiums will rise. It is that **income protection is worth reviewing before it is needed**.

## What the latest insurance results show

Great-West Lifeco reported that its Canadian Group Benefits **base earnings fell 35% year over year**, from $175 million in the third quarter of 2025 to $114 million in the third quarter of 2026. The company attributed the decline primarily to less favourable long-term disability experience. This is a change in the insurer's earnings—not a claim that the number of disability claims rose 35%.

Manulife also identified unfavourable Canadian Group Insurance claims experience in its third-quarter results, even as its Canadian core earnings increased 3% year over year. Manulife described group benefits claims experience as challenging and announced additional support for eligible members receiving disability benefits.

These findings concern particular insurers and reporting periods. They do **not** establish a uniform nationwide increase in disability claims across every insurer or employer plan.

**Sources:** [Great-West Lifeco Q3 2026 report](https://www.powercorporation.com/media/uploads/companies/reports/bgreat-west-lifeco-2026-q3-en.pdf) and [Manulife Q3 2026 results](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/manulife-reports-third-quarter-2026-results-872018950.html).

## Why disability coverage matters to employees

For many Canadians, the ability to earn an income is central to paying the mortgage or rent, buying groceries, saving for retirement and supporting family members.

Employer-sponsored disability benefits can provide valuable protection, but the details vary. Employees should understand:

- **How much income is replaced:** Benefits are often calculated as a percentage of eligible earnings and may be subject to a monthly maximum.
- **When payments begin:** Short-term and long-term disability plans can have different waiting or elimination periods.
- **How long benefits can continue:** The benefit period and definition of disability matter.
- **What counts as income:** Bonuses, commissions and other variable earnings may be treated differently under a plan.
- **Whether coverage continues after leaving a job:** Group coverage generally depends on plan eligibility and employment status.

A benefits booklet is a useful starting point, but policy wording and plan provisions determine actual eligibility.

## The exposure for self-employed professionals and business owners

Business owners face an additional challenge: an extended absence can affect both personal income and business operations.

Consider a consultant, contractor, incorporated professional or owner-operator whose revenue depends heavily on their day-to-day work. If they cannot work, they may still face rent, loan payments, payroll, software subscriptions and other fixed expenses.

Individual disability insurance and business overhead expense coverage can address different needs. The former may help replace eligible personal income; the latter may help with qualifying ongoing business expenses, subject to policy terms. Neither should be assumed to cover every loss.

Owners with employees should also examine their group benefits arrangements, particularly during renewal discussions. Claims experience can influence pricing, but future premium changes depend on many factors, including the insurer, plan design, claims history and negotiations. A broad increase is **not guaranteed**.

## Five questions worth asking now

1. **If my income stopped tomorrow, how long could my emergency savings cover essential expenses?**
2. **Do I have short-term and long-term disability coverage—and do I know the waiting periods?**
3. **Would the maximum monthly benefit actually cover my household obligations?**
4. **If I own a business, what happens to operating costs and client relationships during an extended absence?**
5. **When was my income-protection strategy last reviewed alongside my investments, debt and retirement goals?**

## The bigger financial-planning lesson

A financial plan is not only about how much wealth you accumulate. It is also about whether the plan can continue when life does not go as expected.

Disability insurance is one part of that conversation, alongside emergency savings, workplace benefits, debt management, business continuity planning and other protection strategies. Coverage needs differ based on income, dependants, existing benefits, health, occupation and financial resources.
