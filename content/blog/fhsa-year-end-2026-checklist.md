# FHSA Before Year-End: Five Questions Canadian First-Time Buyers Should Ask

**November 19, 2026 | Legacy Wealth Advisory**

**Planning to buy a first home in 2027—or still deciding whether homeownership is right for you?** The First Home Savings Account (FHSA) is worth understanding before December 31. It can combine a deduction for eligible contributions with tax-free qualifying withdrawals, but the details matter.

## 1. Have you opened an FHSA yet?

For eligible Canadian residents, opening a first FHSA starts the account's participation period and allows participation room to begin accumulating. Room does not accumulate for years before the first FHSA is opened. Eligibility includes age and first-time home-buyer conditions; living in a home owned by a spouse or common-law partner may affect eligibility to open an account.

## 2. How much can you contribute?

The standard annual FHSA participation room starts at **$8,000** in the year the first account is opened, subject to the **$40,000 lifetime contribution-and-transfer limit**. Up to **$8,000 of unused participation room** can generally carry forward, subject to the rules. Your actual available room depends on when you opened your first FHSA, prior contributions and transfers, withdrawals and other adjustments.

**Illustration:** Someone who opened an FHSA in 2025, made no contributions or transfers and otherwise has no adjustments could generally have up to **$16,000 of participation room in 2026**. Someone opening their first FHSA in 2026 would generally begin with **$8,000**, not $16,000.

Check your own CRA records and issuer statements before contributing. Overcontributions can lead to tax.

## 3. Are you confusing a contribution with a tax deduction?

Eligible FHSA contributions are generally deductible, but the deduction does not have to be claimed in the same year. An unused deduction may be carried forward. Direct transfers from an RRSP to an FHSA use FHSA participation room but are **not themselves deductible**.

The tax value of a deduction depends on your circumstances. A tax deduction is not the same as a guaranteed investment return.

## 4. Will the money be needed soon?

If you might purchase within a year or two, consider whether your FHSA holdings match that time horizon. A stock-heavy portfolio may decline just when a down payment is needed. Cash, eligible deposits and other lower-volatility options can reduce market risk, although each option has trade-offs such as inflation risk, interest-rate changes and product restrictions.

The FHSA is an account type, not an investment. What you hold inside it matters.

## 5. What happens if you buy—or decide not to buy?

A **qualifying FHSA withdrawal** can be tax-free when all CRA conditions are met, including the first-time-buyer test, a qualifying written purchase or construction agreement, residency and occupancy requirements, and submission of Form RC725 to the issuer. Qualifying withdrawals do not need to be repaid.

An FHSA cannot remain open indefinitely. Its maximum participation period generally ends on December 31 of the year containing the earliest of the **15th anniversary of opening the first FHSA**, the year the holder turns **71**, or the **year following the first qualifying withdrawal**. Subject to the rules, unused property may generally be transferred directly to an RRSP or RRIF on a tax-deferred basis; an ordinary non-qualifying withdrawal is generally taxable.

## A practical year-end checklist

Before making a decision, verify your eligibility, your exact 2026 FHSA participation room, whether you need the funds soon, the investments held in the account and how the FHSA fits alongside your RRSP, TFSA and emergency savings. Avoid contributing simply because December 31 is approaching.
