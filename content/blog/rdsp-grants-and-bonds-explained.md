The Registered Disability Savings Plan (RDSP) helps Canadians with disabilities, and their families, save for long-term financial security. What makes it stand out is the government money that can be added to the plan through **grants** and **bonds**.

## Who can open an RDSP

In general, the beneficiary must:

- be eligible for the **Disability Tax Credit** (DTC),
- be a resident of Canada with a valid Social Insurance Number when the plan is opened, and
- be under age 60 when the plan is opened.

Grants and bonds can be paid into the plan until the end of the year the beneficiary turns **49**.

## The Canada Disability Savings Grant

The grant matches personal contributions. The matching rate depends on the beneficiary's family net income (for adults, this is usually their own income):

- **Family income at or below the annual threshold:** 300% on the first $500 contributed and 200% on the next $1,000. That is up to **$3,500 in grant** for a $1,500 contribution.
- **Family income above the threshold:** 100% on the first $1,000 contributed, for up to **$1,000 in grant**.

The income threshold is indexed each year, so confirm the current figure before planning contributions.

The lifetime maximum for the grant is **$70,000**.

## The Canada Disability Savings Bond

The bond is designed for beneficiaries with lower family incomes, and **no personal contribution is required** to receive it. Eligible beneficiaries can receive up to **$1,000 a year**, with a lifetime maximum of **$20,000**.

## Catching up on missed years

If an RDSP was opened later, unused grant and bond entitlements from up to the previous **10 years** may be carried forward, subject to annual limits. This can make opening a plan worthwhile even for someone who has qualified for the DTC for years.

## The 10-year rule

Government grants and bonds are meant for long-term saving. If money is withdrawn from the plan, some or all of the grants and bonds paid in the previous 10 years may have to be repaid. Timing withdrawals carefully is an important part of RDSP planning.

## How an RDSP fits into a wider plan

Families often look at the RDSP alongside:

- a Henson trust or other trust planning in a will,
- life insurance that can fund a trust or the RDSP, and
- provincial disability benefits. In Ontario, RDSP savings and withdrawals generally do not affect ODSP eligibility.

Because the rules interact, it helps to plan contributions, withdrawals and estate documents together.
