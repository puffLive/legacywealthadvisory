Retirement planning is not just about how much you have saved. When you begin receiving public pensions can also shape your financial future.

## Understand your CPP options

Eligible Canadians can begin CPP between ages 60 and 70, with 65 as the standard reference age. Starting before 65 reduces the monthly pension by 0.6% per month, reaching a 36% reduction at 60. Delaying beyond 65 adds 0.7% per month, reaching a 42% increase at 70.

For illustration, applying these adjustments to a hypothetical $1,000 monthly age-65 pension gives $640 at 60, $1,000 at 65 and $1,420 at 70. This isolates the timing adjustment; actual entitlements depend on contribution history and other factors. It is not a personal benefit estimate.

## Understand your OAS options

Eligible individuals can start OAS at 65 or defer until 70. Deferral increases the monthly pension by 0.6% per month, up to 36%. Neither pension earns further age-based deferral increases after 70.

OAS deferral can also affect access to the Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) and, where applicable, a partner's Allowance. GIS does not increase because OAS is deferred. OAS recovery tax may apply at higher incomes.

## Consider the bigger picture

A larger monthly pension does not automatically mean a better outcome. Delaying means forgoing payments in the meantime. Health, longevity, immediate expenses, employment, taxes and other retirement assets can all influence the trade-off.

Consider how public pensions fit alongside workplace pensions, RRSPs, RRIFs and TFSAs. Reviewing different starting ages and projected cash flows can help clarify the options.
