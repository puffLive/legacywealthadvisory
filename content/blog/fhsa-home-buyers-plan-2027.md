A down payment is only one part of preparing to buy a home. How you accumulate it, access it and manage your finances after closing can make a meaningful difference.

Two Canadian programs deserve attention: the First Home Savings Account (FHSA) and the RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan (HBP).

## 1. Understand the FHSA

Eligible savers receive $8,000 of participation room when they first open an FHSA. The standard lifetime limit is $40,000, and up to $8,000 of unused room can generally carry forward. Contributions are normally deductible, while qualifying home-purchase withdrawals are tax-free and do not require repayment.

For a potential 2026 deduction, contributions must be made by December 31, 2026. Contributions in early 2027 cannot be deducted for 2026. Direct RRSP-to-FHSA transfers use FHSA room but do not generate a new deduction.

## 2. Understand the Home Buyers’ Plan

The HBP allows eligible participants to access up to $60,000 from their RRSPs for a qualifying purchase without immediate income tax when the rules are met. Unlike an FHSA qualifying withdrawal, the amount generally must be repaid over 15 years.

Under current temporary relief, someone making their first HBP withdrawal in 2027 generally begins required repayments in 2032. A missed required repayment is generally included in taxable income, and designated repayments are not deductible.

## 3. Can the two programs work together?

Yes. Eligible purchasers can use both programs for the same qualifying home if they meet the conditions for each withdrawal. For illustration, someone with $25,000 available for a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and $35,000 eligible for the HBP could access $60,000 in total. The $35,000 HBP amount would still carry repayment obligations.

This is an illustration, not a recommended withdrawal amount.

## 4. Plan beyond the down payment

Closing costs, moving expenses, emergency reserves, mortgage payments and future RRSP repayments all compete for household cash flow. Money needed for a near-term purchase may also require a different investment risk profile than money intended for retirement decades away.

The goal is not simply to access the largest possible down payment. It is to understand how homeownership fits into a sustainable financial plan.
