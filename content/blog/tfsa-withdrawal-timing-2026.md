# TFSA Withdrawals Before Year-End: Why Timing Matters

As December approaches, many Canadians review their savings, investments and registered accounts. One detail worth understanding is how a Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA) handles withdrawals and recontributions.

A TFSA offers flexibility, but the timing of a withdrawal can affect when the same amount can be contributed again without exceeding available room.

## A withdrawal does not immediately restore contribution room

When you withdraw money from a TFSA, the amount withdrawn is generally added back to your contribution room **on January 1 of the following calendar year**, not on the day you withdraw it.

For example, suppose someone has already used all available TFSA contribution room and withdraws $5,000 in December 2026. They generally cannot put that $5,000 back in December without creating an excess contribution. The withdrawal amount is added back to their available room on January 1, 2027, along with the new annual room for that year, if eligible.

The important distinction: withdrawing money and recontributing it are separate transactions with separate contribution-room consequences.

## The annual limit is not everyone's available room

The TFSA annual dollar limit for **2026 is $7,000**, but an individual's actual available room may be higher or lower. Unused room can carry forward, while prior contributions, withdrawals and eligibility affect the calculation.

The Canada Revenue Agency recommends calculating room using personal records and financial institution statements, rather than relying solely on the CRA account display, which may not reflect recent transactions.

## Multiple TFSAs share one limit

Opening a second TFSA does not create a second contribution limit. Contributions across all of a person's TFSAs count toward the same available room.

Moving funds between institutions also requires care. A **direct transfer arranged between TFSA issuers** generally does not affect contribution room, whereas withdrawing funds yourself and depositing them into another TFSA counts as a new contribution.

## What about investments inside the TFSA?

A TFSA is an account structure, not a particular investment. Depending on the institution and product, it may hold cash or eligible investments. Investment decisions should reflect when the money may be needed, the level of risk that is appropriate, and how the TFSA fits with other savings and financial obligations.

For money needed in the near term, understanding liquidity and possible market fluctuations is particularly important.

## Four questions to review before December 31

- Have I confirmed my available TFSA contribution room using my own transaction records?
- Am I planning a withdrawal that I may want to replace soon?
- Am I moving money between institutions, and would a direct transfer be more appropriate?
- Does the investment mix inside my TFSA still match the purpose and timeline for this money?

A year-end TFSA review is about more than maximizing a contribution. It is an opportunity to make sure savings decisions support the broader financial plan.
